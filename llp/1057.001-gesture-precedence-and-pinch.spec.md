## 1. The precedence table

A static rule, derived from the tree and its rows. Each host implements it
with its own mechanism. It is not a runtime structure and authors cannot
change it.

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | The system's own gestures keep their edges (back swipe, home indicator). An authored swipe yields the first 20 pt from the leading edge. |
| 2 | Scroll wins on every axis `touch-action` leaves to it. `touch-action` intersects from the hit node up through the scroll container, as CSS does. |
| 3 | The innermost node with a matching recognizer wins; its ancestors do not also fire (DOM bubbling, as `press` does). A text input or control between the hit node and an ancestor's recognizer keeps the contact for itself; a `press` handler between keeps it from an ancestor's swipe or drag binding, and from an ancestor's `pan` only within the slop: the pan that begins takes the contact and the press, as a draggable element's pointer listeners hear a drag that starts on a button inside it (kanban F6, 2026-10-04). |
| 4 | On one node, one contact: drag binding (reorder > transform > height) > `pan` > `swiperight` > `swipe` (§8.4) > long press / `contextmenu` > `press`. |
| 5 | Double click follows the web: `press` fires on each click, `dblclick` after the second, with no delay. |
| 6 | Exactly one built-in simultaneity: pinch with pan on a transform binding. It is host-owned; authors cannot request simultaneity. |

**Candidate order.** Rules 3 and 4 together order a contact's candidates:
innermost first, then by rule 4 on one node. When the contact crosses its slop,
the first candidate whose axis rule accepts the movement begins; candidates
before it that refused fall away, and nothing after it runs. A drag that begins
cancels the armed `press`.

| host | mechanism |
|---|---|
| web | independent listeners on each element; bubbling puts the innermost first. A swipe, which does not capture at down, marks its pointer `pending` in `exact.contacts` until it claims or refuses; an ancestor's pan, transform or height drag that would capture waits for that verdict. A recognizer that begins stops propagation and suppresses the click, the pan's included |
| macOS | `NodeView.mouseDown` arms every recognizer (`MouseChainMac.swift`), which orders the armed candidates by depth, then rule 4; `mouseDragged` and `mouseUp` offer the event in that order and the first taker keeps it |
| Linux | `pointer_down` collects the candidates and orders them the same way (`presenter/contact.rs`); at the slop the first that accepts begins |
| iOS | UIKit's recognizers and their delegate predicates (`NodeViewIOS.swift`, `GesturePrecedenceIOS.swift`): `ScrollView.gestureRecognizerShouldBegin` for rule 2; an ancestor's drag `shouldRequireFailureOf` a descendant's (rule 3; never of a pinch, which stays possible with one finger); `shouldReceive` refuses a touch under a nested press or editor to the drags; the pinch pair's `shouldRecognizeSimultaneouslyWith` for rule 6 |

**Rule 3's boundary** holds on every host: a `press` handler, a control or a
text input between the contact and an ancestor's drag keeps the contact from
it, so a drag that starts on a button in a swipe row does not swipe
(web: `pressable` in `motion-glue.js` and the pan's check in `input-glue.js`;
iOS: `pressBoundary`). A `pan` passes a `press` (not a control or text input):
the web's pan neither captures nor prevents the pointer until it begins, AppKit
and Linux arm it under the press, and UIKit's pan receives the touch and
cancels the press when it recognizes. **Rule 4's press** arms on a `pan` node on AppKit too
and fires when the pan never leaves the slop; a pan that begins cancels it.

**Declared.** On iOS the order of recognizers on *one* view that cross their
thresholds in the same touch event is UIKit's and undocumented, so rule 4's
tie order on a single node is intended, not enforced; across nodes rule 3 is a
failure requirement. iOS arbitration is checked by unit tests that set
recognizer phases and by delegate predicates. Those tests do not establish
real-touch scroll or long-press arbitration.

**UIKit press forwarding, issue [408](https://github.com/ccheever/exact2/issues/408),
2026-10-10.** A plain text hit beneath an Exact-inserted horizontal
`ScrollView` must reach its authored ancestor's press. On down, a non-canvas
view captures each responder hop across that wrapper; moves, end and cancel
follow the captured contact. Weak responder identity, incarnation and the
downstream contact record prevent delivery to a replacement view or contact.
Retirement, reparenting, disable and inertness cancel armed feedback. A canvas
receives every touch directly in all four phases, without the single-press
contact gate, so its input can track multiple fingers. A child hit inside a
surface control follows its captured responder hop for move, up and cancel,
even though the control's canvas path keeps no press contact. A touch on an overflow child of a surface control is forwarded as one contact; a second finger on that child is not tracked. The original hit,
recognizers, scroll policy and geometry stay UIKit's.

The touched node makes the single `focusForPress` decision: a plain button
takes no touch focus, a descendant that asked for it keeps it, and
`retainFocus` preserves editing. Press acceptance resolves before focus
callbacks can move the owner; a callback that retires the hit cancels the
armed ancestor before activation. Unhandled ground reaches the session's
blur path.

**Acceptance.** `PressFocusIOSTests` covers the scrolled text hit, forwarding
across two wrappers, cancellation and late callbacks, replacement contacts,
retirement and reparenting, nearer and inline handlers, focus and blank ground.
`NodePoolIOSTests` covers a retired forwarding row. `SurfaceControlIOSTests`
covers two canvas fingers through separate downs, moves and ups without cancel,
and a surface control's child hit through move, up and cancel.
The normal iPad fixture accepted gutter and text taps, horizontal and vertical
scroll without a press, a text tap after scrolling, long-press preview and
Detail, and a popover with its focused editor. The unchanged web fixture
accepted gutter and text clicks, a drag without activation, context preview
and Detail. Physical hardware, other iOS versions and normal UI nested-handler
counters remain unaccepted.

**Evidence.** `bun scripts/smoke.mjs <host>` step 11b drives
`contract/corpus/precedence.contract` (a swipe row with a button inside a
panning surface) on raw input: horizontal drag, vertical drag, a drag from the
button, a tap on it. Charlie approved this case as apparatus (2026-09-27).

## 2. `touch-action`: CSS's whole vocabulary

`TouchAction` in `kernel/tables/schema.json` is CSS's grammar, `auto | none |
[ [ pan-x | pan-left | pan-right ] || [ pan-y | pan-up | pan-down ] ||
pinch-zoom ] | manipulation`, in CSS's canonical serialization order: 34
values. The first 13 keep their wire bytes; `pinch-zoom` and the multi-value
forms follow. An author writes the canonical order (`pan-y pinch-zoom`, not
`pinch-zoom pan-y`); the compiler's diagnostic lists every value.

`pinch-zoom` is the browser's zoom, not the app's pinch (Astra's review): a
value that allows it leaves a two-finger pinch to the platform, and an app
pinch (§4) needs the handle's effective value to exclude it. `TouchAction::
pinch_zoom()` answers that (`auto`, `manipulation`, or a value naming
`pinch-zoom`); `TouchAction::pans()` drops `pinch-zoom` for the hosts whose pan
rules match whole values (Linux's swipe admission).

| host | what it does with the row |
|---|---|
| web | emits it as CSS; the browser decides |
| iOS | `NodeView.allowsTouchPan` splits the value into its pan tokens, so the scroll view's intersection (rule 2) ignores `pinch-zoom`; the transform pinch recognizer begins only where a node from the hit view up excludes `pinch-zoom` (§4) |
| macOS | swipe admission reads the pan tokens; trackpad magnify is not a touch and is not governed by the row (as the browser's `ctrlKey` wheel is not) |
| Linux | swipe admission and the retained region's swipe policy match `pans()` |

## 3. One velocity estimator, one threshold table

**Velocity.** `VelocityTracker` (recency-weighted least squares over 100 ms,
`motion/src/velocity.rs`) is the estimator wherever the platform measures
none. The engine keeps one per live hold, fed by every value the hold is given
(`begin_*`, `update_hold`, `update_transform_hold`), and answers
`Engine::hold_velocity(token, now)`; an ended or replaced hold has none.

| path | velocity |
|---|---|
| transform pair, web and Apple | the engine's: op 13's three velocity slots must be zero, the host fills `TransformRelease`'s `vx, vy, vscale` from `hold_velocity`, and the reply carries them as `velocity` for the two hold ends. Swift's `TransformDragVelocity` and the JS endpoint estimator are gone. |
| `swiperight`, web | motion op 10 (`release-measured`) releases at the engine's estimate; the JS estimator is gone |
| `swiperight`, macOS | `exact_hold_end(…, cancel: 2, …)` releases at the engine's estimate; `MouseSwipe`'s last-sample difference is gone |
| `swiperight`, iOS | UIKit's pan velocity, times the resistance past the knee (the platform measures it) |
| Linux | `VelocityTracker` on the contact's hold, as before |

**Constrained displays.** The height binding's display is CSS-clamped
(`min-height`/`max-height`), so the held value can outrun what is shown. The
host records the shown height into the same hold with `Engine::track_hold`
(web: motion op 12 after each move, at the move's instant; Apple: after each
update's layout, from `height_catch`), and `VelocityTracker::push` lets a
sample at the same instant replace the held one. The release then uses the
engine's estimate: web op 9's velocity slot must be zero, and
`exact_height_drag_release` lost its velocity argument. Swift's
`HeightDragVelocity` and the JS height estimator are gone. Web Arrange's
source row releases at the engine's estimate over its hold (the packet's
velocity slots must be zero); Apple Arrange keeps UIKit's or its own and
Linux its contact tracker (`QUEUE.md`).

**Thresholds.** `exact_motion::gesture` holds the values exact2 defines
itself: `SWIPE_KNEE` 64, `SWIPE_RESISTANCE` 0.2, `SWIPE_EDGE` 20, `SLOP` 4,
and `swipe_displacement`. Linux calls them; Apple reads `exact_gesture_constant(i)`
(`Gesture.knee` etc.); the web asks motion op 11 (`gesture`) once. One slop,
4 px, on AppKit, Linux and the web's drags (swipe, height, transform; they
were 8); the web's `pan` keeps the literal because a pan-only plan links no
motion export to ask (LLP 1047). UIKit's pans keep their own hysteresis.

## 4. Pinch on the photo binding

No new Contract surface: `transformDragFor` admits a two-finger pinch where
the host has one, and `transformrelease(x, y, scale, vx, vy, vscale)` already
carries it. It lives inside the existing paired hold (`begin_transform_hold`
and the 120-byte v2 wire): the pair's move carries a scale, and nothing else
changed in the engine or the runner.

**Focal point.** Every sample is the pair anchored at the contact's focal
point (the pinch centroid, the pointer for a trackpad): with the anchor's
value `(x₀, y₀, s₀)`, focal point `F₀` and the current `F`, both relative to
the clip's centre, and the pinch factor `k` since the anchor,

    x = Fx − k·(F₀x − x₀),  y = Fy − k·(F₀y − y₀),  s = k·s₀

so the content under the fingers stays under them and translate follows the
centroid (`k = 1` is a pan). The anchor moves whenever the contact changes:
a recognizer starts or stops, a finger lands or lifts. That absorbs UIKit's
centroid jump and lets a lifted pinch pan on. `TransformDragPosition.focused`
(Swift) and `position` in `motion-glue.js` are the one formula.

**One release.** Whatever mix of pan and pinch the contact was, it ends once:
the terminal sample and time are preflighted whole, the action runs while both
tokens are live, then each surviving original token ends (LLP 1002 D4). The
terminal payload stays finite (the host refuses before the action otherwise);
`vscale` is the engine's measured scale velocity (§3), no longer zero.

**Bounds.** The binding has none of its own; the consumer's release action
clamps. interaction-gallery's `releasePhoto` adopts the released scale held
between Fit (1) and 4× and clamps pan against that zoom; its Fit, 2× and
Center buttons stay the accessible alternative that needs no gesture.

| host | recognition |
|---|---|
| web | `motion-glue.js`'s transform path: up to two pointers (Pointer Events; the handle's `touch-action` must exclude `pinch-zoom` or the browser takes the pinch), a trackpad's `ctrl`+`wheel` (Chromium, Firefox; the listener is not passive, so the page does not zoom; the pinch ends 150 ms after the last wheel) and Safari's `gesturestart`/`gesturechange`/`gestureend`; while the photo is zoomed (`scale > 1`) a plain wheel over the handle pans it, as on macOS, ending 150 ms after the last wheel; unzoomed it stays the page's scroll |
| iOS | a `UIPinchGestureRecognizer` beside the binding's pan, which now takes two fingers; `shouldRecognizeSimultaneouslyWith` admits exactly that pair (rule 6); the pinch begins only where a node from the handle up excludes `pinch-zoom` (§2) |
| macOS | `NodeView.magnify(with:)` pinches about the pointer (no `NSMagnificationGestureRecognizer`); while the photo is zoomed (`scale > 1`) a phased two-finger scroll over the handle pans it, as Preview does, and its momentum is swallowed (no decay driver); unzoomed, the scroll stays the scroll view's |
| Linux | none: evdev tracks no multitouch slots and no touchpad gestures (`host/linux/src/input.rs`). Authored zoom controls are the path; the agent's `pinch` answers `unsupported` |

Catching a pair reads the layer's model transform unless Core Animation is
running a curve on it: the render tree's copy can lag the last presented
frame, and a pinch caught right after another read the scale before it.

## 5. The agent: `tap <target> pinch <scale> [at <x> <y>]`

A form of `tap` (DEFERRED §Agent API), not a ninth operation. The fingers
spread from `d` to `d·scale` about the target's middle, or about `at`
(offset from its top-left), over eight steps.

| carrier | delivery |
|---|---|
| web | `platform`: two CDP touch points, injected without touch emulation so the page's `(pointer)` and `(hover)` stay the device's (Charlie, 2026-10-07) |
| macOS | `platform`: magnify events (began, changed ×8, ended) built from a gesture `CGEvent`'s zoom fields (110, 113, 132; AppKit has no public constructor) and sent to the view under the point, as the wheel form is |
| iOS | `recognized`: UIKit synthesizes no touch, so the recognized scale goes through the same paired hold (`TransformDragHold.recognizedPinch`) |
| Linux | `unsupported` |

LLP 1035.003 D1's one contact per session stands; a pinch is one `tap`, not
two contacts. The macOS carrier also carries `tap … dblclick` now, as two real
clicks, the second with `clickCount` 2.

## 6. `panrelease`: a pan's release velocity

Phase 2's item, built 2026-09-28 for its consumer, `apps/spark` (a card
dragged by `pan` and flung by its throw; Charlie approved it,
`rules/DEFERRED.md` §Motion). `panrelease=action` appends `(vx, vy)`, the
release velocity in viewport CSS pixels per second, pan's units over time.
It completes `pan`; it is not a phase, and the action commits state as a
`pan` does (LLP 1043.000 D8). No hold, no spring, no engine value.

**When.** Once per contact, and only for a pan that *began* (crossed its
recognition: the 4 px slop, or UIKit's own), after that contact's last `pan`
delta. A contact that never began releases nothing: it is a tap, and rule
4's `press` on the same node fires instead. A node without a `panrelease`
handler is sent nothing; `panrelease` without `pan` never fires.

**Cancel.** A contact cancelled after it began releases at rest, `(0, 0)`:
the app always hears the end of what it started, and a card springs back.
Cancelled means `pointercancel` or a lost capture on the web, UIKit's
`.cancelled`/`.failed`, Escape or the window resigning key on AppKit, and
Linux's `pointer_cancel` (Escape, wheel takeover, disconnection). A node
that is gone or no longer live is sent nothing.

**Velocity** is §3's rule: the platform's where it measures one, the one
estimator elsewhere.

| host | velocity |
|---|---|
| web | the pan's pointer samples, each at its event's own `timeStamp` (so a driven drag under the agent's clock still has a real velocity), go to motion ops 13 (`pan-sample`) and 14 (`pan-velocity`), a `VelocityTracker` in the wasm (`host/web/src/pan_velocity.rs`); ABI kind 28 delivers it. A plan with `panrelease` uses motion (`runner/src/uses.rs`), so the export is linked (LLP 1047 stage 3) |
| iOS | UIKit's `velocity(in:)` at `.ended` |
| macOS | the mouse chain's samples at their `NSEvent` timestamps into a `VelocityTracker` behind the Apple ABI; no Swift estimator |
| Linux | the contact's `VelocityTracker`, fed every sample since down |

**Agent.** No new operation: the contact phases end a pan, `tap <t> down`,
`tap move by <dx> <dy> over <ms>`, `tap up` (or `tap cancel`). Web and
macOS deliver them as platform input, Linux through its presenter; on iOS,
where UIKit synthesizes no touch, a contact on a `pan` node is delivered
`recognized` through the same pan path, its release velocity the tracker's
over the synthesized samples.

**A pause before lifting releases at rest.** The tracker weighs only the
last 100 ms (§3), so a finger that stops and then lifts has no throw left;
UIKit's `velocity(in:)` at `.ended` says the same. That is kept on purpose.
What a driver must not do is invent the pause: the web carrier stamps each
touch event with the contact's own timeline (CDP `timestamp`), so a `tap
up` lands one frame after the last move however long the driver took
between operations, and `tap hold <ms>` is how a pause is said
(`host/web/tests/pan-release-engine.test.mjs`, the real wasm, nothing mocked).


## 7. A swipe beside the back gesture on iOS (2026-10-04, Signal Clone)

A tester found the clone "hard to swipe back sometimes". With HID touches on
the iOS 27 simulator, a back swipe from the edge of a chat became a reply,
and others did nothing. Three fixes:
- **A `swiperight` row judges the edge where its finger landed.** It records
  its first touch in `gestureRecognizer(_:shouldReceive:)`, as the pop
  recognizers already did (LLP 1080.000 §11). The pan's location less its
  translation at `shouldBegin` leaves out the travel before recognition: a
  swipe from x = 2 read as starting at about 30, past the 20-point edge.
- **A `swiperight` row waits for the screen-edge pop to fail**
  (`interactivePopGestureRecognizer`), as UIKit's swipe actions and Signal's
  swipe to reply do: from the edge, back wins. Only that recognizer. iOS
  26's content pop is the same class and already waits on the swipe; waiting
  on it in turn left neither able to begin.
- **The pop's direction rule takes no motion as no evidence against.** UIKit's
  pop recognizers asked `shouldBegin` with zero velocity and zero
  translation, and `velocity.x > |velocity.y|` refused every such back swipe.
  Zero velocity now falls back to the travel so far, and a zero vector is
  allowed (still refused past the edge over a `swiperight` node, and over a
  canvas). UIKit's pop recognizers judge the direction themselves: vertical
  drags from x = 10 and x = 150 on a pushed screen without `swiperight`
  rows scrolled and did not pop (simulator, HID).

Proofs: `NavigationRulesTests.testAPanYieldsToASwipeRightNodePastTheEdge`
(the zero vector, and `NavigationRules.popDirection`'s fallback to travel),
`GesturePrecedenceIOSTests.testASwipeWaitsForTheEdgePopOnly`, and HID drives
on the clone (iOS 27 only; iOS 18's screen-edge path is not yet driven): edge swipes from x = 4, 12
and 30 over a transcript go back, a swipe from x = 60 on a bubble replies,
vertical drags scroll.


## 8. `swipe`: a discrete swipe in the directions a node names (2026-10-30)

Built for its consumer, the chat template's composer bar: a quick flick down on
the bar, the field in it included, ends editing, and the keys go down at once
on their own animation, as a UIKit app's input bar does with a
`UISwipeGestureRecognizer`. `swiperight` is not that: it is a drag that follows
the finger past a knee and releases, and only to the right. `swipe` is the
platform's discrete recognizer: nothing follows the finger; the action runs
once, when the platform says a swipe happened.

```
column id="composer-bar" swipe=hideKeys swipe-direction="down"
  …
  row id="composer-pill"
    textarea id="composer" field-sizing="content" max-height=…
    …
action hideKeys
  blur("composer")
```

The bar is the last child of the conversation's column, under the transcript
and inside no scroller; its field grows with its text to a maximum and then
scrolls. A flick down that starts on the bar, or on the field while its text
fits, hides the keys. One that starts on the field while its text overflows
scrolls the field, as any scroller wins (§8.4).

### 8.1 Surface

- `swipe=action` runs once per contact, when the platform recognizes a swipe
  in one of the node's directions. The action may take a `SwipeEvent
  { direction: string }`: `"left"`, `"right"`, `"up"` or `"down"`, the one
  recognized. It takes it as `contextmenu`'s takes `PointerEvent`.
- `swipe-direction` names the directions, space-separated, one or more of
  `left`, `right`, `up` and `down`. They are physical, as UIKit's are: a
  right-to-left layout does not mirror them. Required with `swipe`.
- `touch-count`, a whole number of at least 1, is how many touches the swipe
  takes; a recognizer for one count hears no other. Omitted, the swipe is the
  platform's own: UIKit's one-finger swipe, Android's fling, on the Mac
  AppKit's swipe as the person's "Swipe between pages" settings make it (a
  trackpad's two or three fingers, a Magic Mouse's one, §8.2).
- `swipe-direction` and `touch-count` are kernel props, lowered as
  `intersect-margin` is (`contract/lower/src/tags.rs`). A literal is checked
  when the plan is built: `lower-swipe-direction` for no direction or a word
  that is none, `lower-swipe-count` for a count that is not a whole number of
  at least 1, `lower-swipe-unused` for either without `swipe`. A computed value that
  is none of these is journalled once and leaves the swipe off until it is
  valid again. A node may not have `swiperight` and a one-touch `swipe` that
  names `right` (`lower-swipe-twice`); other counts recognize other contacts.
- `swipe=` is shorthand for LLP 1057.000's future discrete leaf,
  `swipe(direction:, touchCount:) on end <action>`, whose one terminal
  payload is the direction. It is not a filter on a drag's release: that would
  change both recognition and precedence.

### 8.2 Recognition

The platform recognizes; the engine follows (LLP 1002 D4). Nothing is held,
sprung or sampled per frame, so `swipe` alone links no motion module
(`runner/src/uses.rs`).

| host | recognizer | counts |
|---|---|---|
| iOS | one `UISwipeGestureRecognizer` per named direction on the node's view (one recognizer given several directions does not say which it saw), `numberOfTouchesRequired` = the count | any |
| macOS | AppKit's swipe, by the person's "Swipe between pages" settings, whichever device makes it, reaching the node through the responder chain under the pointer as the window's Back reaches its view (`BackSwipe`, `BackMac.swift`). Two paths. `swipe(with:)`, which a trackpad's three-finger swipe makes when that is the setting; its direction is AppKit's signs: `deltaX` > 0 left, < 0 right; `deltaY` > 0 up, < 0 down. And AppKit's swipe tracking from phased scroll events (`NSEvent.isSwipeTrackingFromScrollEventsEnabled`), which a trackpad's two-finger scroll and a Magic Mouse's one-finger scroll make when theirs is the setting (AppKit gives the view no device, so the two are one path). AppKit routes scrolling up the responder chain from the view under the pointer, so the nodes and each enclosing scroll view have the event first, and Exact's scrollers decide where a phased gesture goes by their own routing and latch (`ChainingScrollView.routing`, `latched`, after Chrome's): the innermost that can scroll that way takes it, an axis with `overscroll-behavior` `contain` or `none` drops it, and only a gesture every scroller under the pointer passes on goes up. That one reaches the page's scroll view, an `NSScrollView`, which hands the root view's receiver, where the window's Back is tracked today (`ExactViewMac.scrollWheel(with:)`, `BackSwipe.scroll`), a gesture whose `.began` moves more sideways than not, while the root wants horizontal swipe tracking (`wantsScrollEventsForSwipeTracking(on:)`), and only that `.began`: AppKit decides once, at the gesture's start, so a gesture that set out vertically, or whose `.began` carries no delta, reaches no receiver whatever it turns to (an `NSScrollView` on macOS 27 given each of these). No node takes a scroll event for a swipe on its way up. The receiver picks, by rule 3, the innermost node whose swipe names the direction on the hit path at that `.began`, else the window's Back, and tracks it with `trackSwipeEvent` to its end wherever the pointer then goes (a Magic Mouse moves it during the gesture); the swipe fires when the tracking ends on the side it set out for. The root wants horizontal tracking while a node under the pointer has a left or right swipe, or Back is possible. The direction is the fingers': `scrollingDeltaX` is reported as the person scrolls, so with natural scrolling (`isDirectionInvertedFromDevice` true) a positive value is the fingers moving right, and without it, left. That path is horizontal only: vertical travel scrolls. A mouse's wheel, whose events are not phased, and its button drags make no swipe. | omitted only: AppKit reports no finger count, so a node that names one never fires |
| web | none: the web has no swipe of its own, and Exact makes none (§8.3) | none |
| Android | `GestureDetector`'s `onFling`, the platform's swipe, fed the carrier's own touches whole (`ExactView.kt`, on the UI thread, every pointer of each `MotionEvent`), with `ViewConfiguration`'s touch slop and fling velocities, as the carrier's scrolling takes them (`Scrolling.kt`). It fires at the lift, as Android's fling does: a fling whose touch the carrier's scrolling took is none; one it did not take goes to the presenter, its velocity's larger axis and that axis's sign the direction. The presenter dispatches it to the innermost node on the hit path saved at the touch's first down whose swipe names that direction (rule 3), unless one of its own gestures took the contact (rule 4). The presenter's own contact is one pointer and a second ends it (`ExactView.kt` sends a pointer's coming or going as a cancel); the saved path and the touch's fling outlive that end. | omitted: the detector's own flings, a hand-off between fingers included; a count: a fling counts when the most pointers the touch had down at once is that count |
| Linux, Windows | none: their platforms give their one pointer (a mouse, and on Linux a tablet's pen or a touchscreen's first touch) no swipe, and Exact makes none | none |
| tvOS | the same `UISwipeGestureRecognizer`s on the node's view, hearing the remote's touch surface (its indirect touches, `allowedTouchTypes`), as UIKit offers for the Siri Remote; they hear it while the focus is in the node. Whether the focus also moves at the same swipe is UIKit's, as for any app's swipe recognizer, and a test on the Apple TV simulator records it; directions the node does not name move the focus as before | omitted or 1: the remote's surface is one touch, so a larger count never fires |

### 8.3 The web

The web has no swipe of its own, and Exact makes none: a node's `swipe`
never fires there, as on Linux and Windows. An app that needs the effect on
the web gives it a control, as §8.6 asks anyway. `swiperight` keeps its own
drag on the web (§7).

### 8.4 Precedence

A swipe is discrete: pending from the first touch until it recognizes or
fails. Rules 1–4 apply, with this order of `swipe` against the rest:

- **Continuous gestures do not wait for it.** Scroll, a `pan`, a drag binding
  and `swiperight`, on the node or an ancestor, begin at their own thresholds;
  one that begins takes the contact, and the pending swipe fails. On iOS the
  swipe recognizer, through its delegate's
  `gestureRecognizer(_:shouldRequireFailureOf:)`, requires the failure of the
  pan of each scroll view on the touch's hit path (from the view it landed on
  up to the window, an editor under the node included) that admits travel in
  the swipe's direction from the touch's start. That is the pan's own
  admission, both halves of it: UIKit's, that the view scrolls on that axis
  (its content overflows there, or it bounces there: `alwaysBounceVertical`,
  which Exact sets on some scrollers whose content fits, `NodeViewIOS.swift`);
  and Exact's, the same predicate its `gestureRecognizerShouldBegin` asks.
  For a scroller that is `ScrollView.admitsPan` (`ScrollViewIOS.swift`: rule
  2's `touch-action` from the hit through the scroller, then scroll chaining),
  asked with the swipe's direction; for a textarea, that its text overflows
  (`fits`, `TextAreaIOS.swift`), which is all a textarea's pan asks: it reads
  no `touch-action`, so an overflowing field with `touch-action: none` still
  scrolls. The swipe waits for a required pan to fail, so a pan that begins
  takes the contact and the swipe fails, whatever distance UIKit asks of a
  swipe at that speed (it asks less of a fast one, `UISwipeGestureRecognizer.h`):
  scroll wins by construction. The requirement only orders recognizers that
  would both succeed. A pan it leaves out that begins
  anyway (the finger first went another way) still takes the contact, as any
  recognizer that begins first does; one it names that refuses fails as it
  refuses (UIKit fails a recognizer whose `gestureRecognizerShouldBegin`
  returns false), a few points in. §1's candidate order at the slop does not
  apply to `swipe`: it is never a candidate that begins at the slop.
- **It cancels what has not happened yet.** A swipe that recognizes cancels
  the contact's armed `press` and a long press that has not begun, as a drag
  that begins does (rule 4). A long press that began (UIKit's runs its action
  at `.began`, `ContextGesturesIOS.swift`) has already won: the finger moved
  less than its allowance until then, and a swipe after it fails. A contact
  that never leaves the slop is still a tap.
- **Rule 3, for a text editor, on iOS, for a finger.** A native text editor
  between the hit and the swiped node keeps taps, long presses, selection
  and its own scrolling, but not a finger's swipe that starts on it: the
  swiped node's recognizers receive the editor's direct touches
  (`NodeViewIOS.gestureRecognizer(_:shouldReceive:)` admits a touch of type
  `.direct` for swipe recognizers). A pointer's or pencil's contact on an
  editor stays the editor's, since its drag selects text, and starts no
  swipe there. A finger's selection is the editor's whatever its timing: a
  drag of the caret or of a selection handle, a double or triple tap's
  selection and a long press's all change the editor's selection as they
  act. So any change of the editor's selection while the contact is down
  fails its pending swipe before it can recognize, whatever the selection
  comes back to: the host latches the change for the contact
  (`textViewDidChangeSelection`, `TextAreaIOS.swift`, and
  `textFieldDidChangeSelection` for a one-line field, which UIKit calls as
  the change is made), and while it is set the recognizers' delegate answers
  false in `gestureRecognizerShouldBegin`, which UIKit asks before a
  discrete recognizer recognizes and which then fails it. The latch clears
  when the contact ends. A swipe refused so never recognized, so it cancels
  none of the editor's touches (`cancelsTouchesInView`), and a handle keeps
  dragging however far it goes. A touch that comes down on a
  field moves no caret before it lifts, so a flick leaves the selection as
  it was. An editor that scrolls on the swipe's axis takes the contact by
  the scroll rule above: the composer's field, while it overflows. On
  Android a touch on a native field goes to the field alone
  (`ExactView.kt`), so there a swipe starts only outside a field.
- **The back gestures.** The leading 20 points still yield to the system's
  Back (rule 1), and each swipe recognizer waits for the screen-edge pop to
  fail, as a `swiperight` row does (§7). A node with a one-touch swipe in the
  leading-to-trailing direction (right, or left in a right-to-left layout) is
  protected from the content pop as a `swiperight` node is
  (`NavigationIOS.swift`), judged at the touch's first location, recorded in
  `shouldReceive` (§7), and only when that swipe is eligible on the touch's hit
  path: the same boundary and configuration checks as its recognizer's
  (enabled, a valid configuration, no `press` or control between the hit and
  the node that keeps the contact). On macOS `swipe(with:)` goes up the
  responder chain from the view under the pointer, so a node's swipe has it
  before the window's Back (`ExactViewMac.swipe(with:)`) in the directions it
  names and passes the others up; a tracked scroll reaches the root's
  receiver after the scroll views, which picks the node's swipe before Back
  (§8.2).
- **Nested swipes (rule 3).** The innermost node whose swipe matches the
  contact's direction and count wins. On iOS an outer node's recognizer for
  a direction and count requires the failure of each recognizer for the same
  direction and count on a node between the hit and it
  (`gestureRecognizer(_:shouldRequireFailureOf:)`); an inner node that
  matches neither does not hold it up. On Android the presenter gives
  the fling to the innermost match on the contact's saved hit path. On
  macOS the responder chain is that order.
- **Same node, same event.** As §1 declares, UIKit's order between
  recognizers on one view that cross their thresholds in one touch event is
  its own and undocumented; the order above is intended on iOS and enforced
  elsewhere.

### 8.5 Wire and runner

ABI kind 60, `swipe`: UTF-8 `left`, `right`, `up` or `down`
(`host/apple/include/exact.h`, `host/web/src/abi.rs`; `Event::Swipe` decoded in
`Event::of_host_kind`). The plan format gains `EventKind::Swipe` at its next
free ordinal; the plan's numbers and the ABI's are separate (plan 60 is
`intersect`). A host dispatches only for the configuration it is running: each host keeps
its recognizers per node with the directions and count they were made for,
and on a change of either (or a computed value going invalid), or when the
node's view is replaced, removes them and makes new ones, which fails a
pending contact's swipe; the new ones take no part in that contact. A recognition from
a recognizer that is no longer the node's current one is dropped by the host
before dispatch and journalled; that identity stays inside the host, and the
payload stays the direction. Dispatch, which knows the node, also checks the
direction against its current `swipe-direction` and drops a mismatch the same
way, not as an error batch. The web hosts bind no `swipe`: the plan carries
it and nothing there fires it.

#### 8.6 Accessibility

A swipe adds nothing to the accessibility tree. VoiceOver's own swipes move
its focus; a person makes an app's swipe under it with VoiceOver's standard
gesture (double-tap and hold until three rising tones, then swipe: "Learn
VoiceOver gestures", iPhone and iPad User Guides), and with Voice Control by
its "Swipe" commands. A swipe is a path-based
gesture, so its effect must also be reachable with a single pointer without
one, by a tap or a click, unless the path is essential (WCAG 2.2, 2.5.1
Pointer Gestures), as the keyboard
recipe's Done button puts the keyboard away
(`docs/recipes/keyboard-and-focus.md`); Exact makes none, and an author who
binds `swipe` owns that obligation. The composer's swipe adds a second path to
what the transcript's interactive keyboard dismissal (`keyboardDismissMode`)
already does by a drag. Neither supplies a tap or click alternative, and
whether the chat screen gives hiding the keys one is the template's to
decide, in its QUEUE; this section does not claim the swipe supplies one.

### 8.7 The agent

`tap <target> swipe <left|right|up|down> [at <x> <y>]` checks, before
anything is delivered, what the host's own delivery needs, and refuses with
the reason when any fails. Everywhere: the node takes a swipe in that
direction at its count and is the innermost node on the hit path whose swipe
matches them (a nearer match would take the contact, §8.4), and the start
point (the target's middle, or `at`, a
point in it, so the field and the bar can each be the start) is on screen and
not covered.

- On iOS, with the hit-path eligibility a recognized `pan` uses
  (`AgentPanIOS.swift`): the start is outside the system's leading 20 points;
  no `press` or control between the hit and the node keeps the contact; no
  scroll view or continuous recognizer on the hit path would begin on that
  axis (§8.4); and, where the drive moves a finger (the touch runner), the
  path, 120 points in that direction over 100 ms, stays inside the
  window.
- On macOS the drive is a three-finger `swipe(with:)`, the path that reaches
  a node whatever scrolls around it; the setting "two or three fingers"
  keeps both paths live, so the tracked path's arbitration is proven by
  tests rather than the drive. AppKit has no public constructor for the
  event; a gesture `CGEvent` of IOHID's swipe type carries its direction
  mask, as a pinch's carries its zoom (`AgentSwipeMac.swift`), and the view
  under the start point gets it, so it is delivered `platform`, up the real
  responder chain. Its checks: the node is on the responder chain of the
  view under the start point, no view nearer on that chain takes a swipe in
  that direction, and the node names no count. A scroll view on the chain
  does not stand in the way: `swipe(with:)` is a different event from
  scrolling. A tracked two-finger scroll reaches the node only past every
  scroller and node that takes scroll events first (§8.2), which the drive
  does not stand in for.
- On the Android reader, whose driver speaks to the presenter and injects
  none of the platform's touches, it is delivered `recognized`
  (`Presenter::agent_swipe`, `presenter/fling.rs`):
  the fling a touch at the start point would make, at the node's count,
  once that touch would reach the node, by the presenter's own rule: the
  node is hit there, takes the direction at that count, is the innermost
  such on the hit path, and no `press`, control or text input between keeps
  the contact. A hosted field's box is the field's, and the reader's
  scrolling takes a touch before the presenter sees it; the carrier's tests
  prove those halves.
- On the web, Linux and Windows, which make no swipe, the drive refuses,
  saying so;
  on tvOS it is delivered `recognized`, as on iOS, to a node holding the
  focus.

On iOS, where UIKit synthesizes no touch, it is delivered `recognized` at
the start point, which moves nothing, so no path is checked. That drive
proves the wiring, and real-touch tests prove the arbitration. Under
`--touch platform` the touch runner swipes for real with one touch
(`host/apple/touches.swift`), checking the path, and refuses a larger
count.

### 8.8 Vocabulary

`contract vocab swipe` answers the attribute and keeps the concept page it
already has (`contract/cli/src/vocab/concepts.md`), and the page names the
three kinds apart: `swipe`, a discrete swipe; `swiperight`, a drag that follows
the finger and releases; and a list row's swipe actions (`swipeContent`,
`swipeLeading`, `swipeTrailing`).

### 8.9 Proofs, when built

- The compiler's diagnostics; the runner's decoding, dispatch checks and
  dropped stale deliveries, after a change of count too.
- iOS, `GesturePrecedenceIOSTests`: the recognizers' directions and counts;
  their failure requirements on the hit path's scroll views (an overflowing
  editor's and a fitting one's, a bouncing scroller's, one whose
  `touch-action` or chaining refuses the direction, and one that fits) and
  the edge pop; the editor's touches (a direct touch admitted, a pointer's
  and a pencil's not); nested swipes, an inner node matching and one
  matching neither direction nor count; a node's view replaced during a
  contact, whose old recognizers fail and whose new ones take no part until
  the next contact.
- iOS, real touches on the iPhone simulator: the composer, from the bar and
  from a fitting field the keys go down, and from an overflowing field the
  field scrolls and the keys stay; the field's selection, a quick drag of a
  selection handle and of the caret, a handle dragged far and on (the
  test's distance a fixture, not UIKit's),
  a selection that changes and comes back to where it was, and a double
  tap's selection, each keeping the keys up and the selection it made.
- tvOS, on the Apple TV simulator: a remote swipe on a focused node in a
  direction it names fires, and one it does not name moves the focus.
- Android, the carrier's tests: a fling on a swipe node fires its direction
  at the lift; a fling whose touch the carrier's scrolling took fires none; a
  fling on a contact a presenter gesture took fires none; a fling on a field
  fires none; nested nodes give it to the innermost match; with no count, a
  two-finger fling and a fling after a hand-off between fingers fire, on the
  node under the first finger; with a count of 1 they fire none, and with 2
  the two-finger one fires.
- macOS, each path before the window's Back: `swipe(with:)` reaching a node
  inside a scroll view that can still scroll; a tracked phased scroll
  (`BackSwipe`'s injected tracking) delivered through the real responder
  chain from a node inside a scroll view, reaching the node only where that
  scroll view is pinned that way or there is none, a vertical one never,
  left and right each with natural scrolling on and off, a gesture whose
  pointer moves off the node after its first event (as a Magic Mouse's does)
  still the node's, a gesture whose `.began` carries no delta never tracked,
  and a fitting horizontal scroller with `overscroll-behavior: contain`
  around the node, a zoomed transform binding and an authored `wheel`
  handler that prevents the default on the chain each keeping the tracked
  scroll from it.
- The agent's `tap … swipe` on each host, refusals included: on macOS its
  three-finger drive accepted at a node inside a scroll view, a zoomed
  transform and a `wheel` handler alike, as `swipe(with:)` reaches it there;
  on the web, Linux and Windows refused.
