## Summary

On iOS an app has one accent: its window's `tintColor`, usually an AccentColor asset. Any view can override the tint for its subtree, and any view can say how its tint behaves behind a modal: follow the platform (`automatic`), keep its colour (`normal`), or grey (`dimmed`). Every control, symbol and link that uses the tint follows this one cascade, and the HIG relies on it: the accent marks what the user can act on, and greys when a modal makes the screen beneath inert.

Exact has the pieces but not the cascade:
- `accent-color` is inherited, but only controls read it. It never becomes a view's `tintColor`, so a symbol inside `accent-color: red` stays the app's accent.
- `-exact-tint-color` colours one symbol or raster. It is not inherited, and its initial value, `AccentColor`, is the inherited `tintColor`.
- Nothing sets the window's tint, so alerts, sheets and bars stay system blue in an app whose root says red. (Built on the branch: D1.)
- Nothing says how a tint behaves behind a modal.

This RFC makes `accent-color` UIKit's tint cascade, adds `-exact-accent-color-adjust` for `tintAdjustmentMode`, and removes `-exact-tint-color`. A symbol's colour becomes `color`, as LLP 1081 stage 2 already decided, and raster tinting goes: a one-colour icon is a symbol or an SVG in `currentColor`.

## 1. Motivation

**Two rows for one idea.** To tint a section of a screen red today, an author sets `accent-color` for its controls and `-exact-tint-color` on each symbol. A hand-built UIKit screen sets one `tintColor` on the section's view. The two rows also disagree on inheritance: `accent-color` inherits, `-exact-tint-color` does not.

**The window never hears the accent.** UIKit's own chrome (alert and action-sheet buttons, bar items, the back chevron, `UIMenu` checkmarks, text selection) reads the window's tint. An Exact app with `accent-color: red` on its root has red buttons in red-accented screens and blue buttons in its alerts. Someone who knows the platform notices (LLP 1115 D1).

**Dimming has no author control.** The HIG has the accent grey behind an alert or a sheet. UIKit does that with `tintAdjustmentMode`, which a view can also pin: a colour that means something by itself (a recording dot, a brand mark drawn in the tint) can stay as it is. Exact has no row for it, so the only way not to grey was to stop being a tint (a baked colour), which also stops following the accent.

**`-exact-tint-color` names the wrong thing.** For a symbol, LLP 1081 stage 2 already moves the colour to `color`. What remains is a raster's switch: its presence makes a bitmap a template drawn in one colour. A colour row used as a boolean is easy to misread, and no app uses it: the repo's only tinted rasters are two fixtures (`scripts/fixtures/tint.contract`, `host/web-js/conformance/symbols.contract`).

## 2. Decisions

### D1 — The root's `accent-color` is the app's accent

**Built** (`host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/AppAccentIOS.swift`, branch `feat/accent-window-tint`).

- The first root that names a colour of its own (not `AccentColor`, not absent) gives the window its `tintColor`, light and dark. Sheets, alerts, bars, `SystemColor.appTint` and every `AccentColor` follow it, as an AccentColor asset makes them.
- With no such root, the window keeps the tint it had, restored when the accent goes.
- The tint is set before a new tree joins the window, so UIKit does not tell every view its tint changed. The colour report the change causes waits until the batch is in.
- **macOS:** an app's accent is `NSAccentColorName` in Info.plist, read at launch. An authored root accent is not applied to AppKit's chrome; controls keep reading `accent-color` as they do today. **Web:** see D2.

### D2 — A node's `accent-color` is its subtree's tint

**Proposed.**

- **iOS:** a node with an authored `accent-color` sets its view's `tintColor` to it; a node without one leaves `tintColor` nil and inherits, as UIKit does. Controls, symbols drawn in `AccentColor` (D4), links in `LinkText`, carets and selection below follow it with nothing resolved by Exact.
- **The controls' readers stay.** `ControlsIOS`, `ValueControlsIOS`, `SegmentsIOS`, `NavigationTabsIOS`, `MenusIOS`, `SwipeActionsIOS` and the fields read `accent_color` today. They keep doing so where UIKit needs an explicit colour (a `UIButton.Configuration`'s base colour); elsewhere the inherited `tintColor` is enough, and a reader that only repeats it is removed.
- **`AccentColor` resolves per view.** LLP 1095 §9 notes the kernel resolves `AccentColor` to one tint per process. On iOS a view's paint already uses its own tint; a value the kernel resolves for itself (a gradient stop, a reported colour) keeps the app's accent, a limit §3 lists.
- **macOS** has no inherited tint. AppKit controls read `accent-color` as today; a symbol drawn in `AccentColor` uses the nearest authored `accent-color`, resolved by the host.
- **Web:** CSS `accent-color` already inherits and tints form controls, but a browser's `AccentColor` is the UA's accent, not the author's. Both web targets write an authored `accent-color` also as `--exact-accent`, and write the keyword `AccentColor` as `var(--exact-accent, AccentColor)`, so `color: AccentColor` follows the author's accent there too.
- **LLP 1115 D4 is edited to say so.** It read "inherited colours never tint controls … unless the control's own `color`/`accent-color` is set", which could forbid the cascade. What it guards against is a page's text colour leaking into a bar button (d33eb49dd). It now reads: a control's colour is the tint, never an inherited `color`; the tint is `accent-color`, which cascades as UIKit's `tintColor` does; a control takes a `color` only when it is written on the control.

### D3 — Every tint greys behind a modal by default; `-exact-accent-color-adjust` says otherwise

**The default is built** (`feat/accent-window-tint`): no Exact view pins `tintAdjustmentMode`, so the app's accent and a subtree's own both grey behind an alert, as UIKit's do.

**The row is proposed:**
- `-exact-accent-color-adjust: auto | normal | dimmed`, inherited, initial `auto`. The name follows CSS's `print-color-adjust` and `forced-color-adjust`: how the platform may adjust a colour.
- **iOS:** the view's `tintAdjustmentMode`: `auto` is `.automatic` (follow the superview, and the window behind a modal), `normal` keeps the tint's colour, `dimmed` greys it always. Set only on a node that authors it; below, UIKit's own inheritance carries it.
- It adjusts the *tint*: `accent-color`, `AccentColor`, `LinkText`, and what UIKit draws in the tint. A `color` or `background-color` that is not the tint never greys; that is unchanged.
- **macOS, tvOS, the web, Linux:** no platform dims a tint behind a modal, so the row is admitted and draws nothing, declared in LLP 1001.
- The schema row is inherited and enumerated (`enum:AccentColorAdjust`). The compatibility id moves.

**Guidance for agents** (`docs/contract-for-agents.md`): leave it `auto`. Write `normal` only for a tint that means something by itself while a modal is up, which is rare; a status colour should not be a tint at all (`color: -exact-system-green`).

### D4 — A symbol is drawn in `color`

**Accepted in LLP 1081 §4 stage 2, deferred; unchanged here.** A symbol's monochrome colour is its computed `color`. With LLP 1095 D8, `color` starts as `CanvasText`, so an unstyled symbol is the label colour, as SwiftUI's `Image(systemName:)` is. A symbol that should be the accent writes `color: AccentColor`; on iOS that is the inherited `tintColor` (D2), so it follows a subtree's accent and greys behind a modal (D3).

This RFC adds one requirement to stage 2: **a symbol whose `color` is `AccentColor` is drawn as a template in the view's tint**, never as a resolved colour, so UIKit's dimming and a subtree's accent reach it.

### D5 — `-exact-tint-color` is deleted, and raster tinting with it

**Proposed**, replacing LLP 1081 stage 2's "`-exact-tint-color` for raster images only".

- **Why a raster needs a switch at all.** A file cannot say how it wants to be drawn: a photo or a logo shows its own pixels, while a one-colour icon drawn as a PNG is a shape to fill with the current colour (UIKit's `.alwaysTemplate`). Today the presence of `-exact-tint-color` is that switch. Without a switch, an inherited colour would fill every photo.
- **No switch replaces it.** The two ways an icon is made today, an SF Symbol and an SVG whose paint is `currentColor`, already follow `color` (D4; LLP 1095 D8). No app in the repo tints a raster. A raster always shows its own pixels.
- `-exact-tint-color` is removed (`rules/RULES.md`: delete, don't deprecate). The compiler refuses it and names the replacement: on a symbol, `color`; on a raster, a symbol or an SVG in `currentColor`.
- **A native button's symbol** (LLP 1069.011.001 D12): its own colour is `color` on the symbol, where it was `-exact-tint-color`. Absent, it follows the title, as now.
- **Motion:** `transition: -exact-tint-color` and keyframes naming it move to `color`, as LLP 1081 stage 2 already does for symbols.
- **Web:** `--exact-tint` and the raster mask go; a symbol's mask fills with `currentColor` (LLP 1081 stage 2).
- **Fixtures:** `scripts/fixtures/tint.contract` and the conformance page's tinted rasters are deleted with the row.

## 3. Costs

- **Pixels change** where a symbol inside an authored `accent-color` drew in `AccentColor`: it now takes the subtree's accent (D2). And a raster that set `-exact-tint-color` shows its own pixels; its author makes it a symbol or an SVG (D5). A sweep lists both before landing; screenshots are reviewed, not required to match.
- **Two schema changes** (a new row in D3, a removed row in D5), so the compatibility id moves and native hosts need a new binary (LLP 1030 D3a). Pre-1.0, accepted.
- **The web approximation of `AccentColor`** (D2) is Exact's custom property, not the browser's keyword alone.
- **A colour the kernel resolves for itself** (a gradient stop, a colour reported to the runner) takes the app's accent, not a subtree's, since the kernel resolves `AccentColor` once per process (LLP 1095 §9).

## 4. Test cases

Written before the code they test. Each names where it lives.

**D1, the app's accent** (`AppAccentIOSTests`, built):
1. A root with `accent-color: red` gives the window a red `tintColor`; a view below inherits it; restyling the root to blue makes the window blue; a new root with no accent gives the window its own tint back.
2. A root with `accent-color: AccentColor` leaves the window's tint alone.
3. The window's tint is set inside a batch, and the colour report it causes arrives after the batch's last op.

**D2, a subtree's tint** (`AppAccentIOSTests`, new):
4. A node with `accent-color: red` has a red `tintColor`; its child without one inherits red; its sibling with none inherits the app's accent.
5. Removing the node's `accent-color` (a restyle) clears its `tintColor`, and its child follows the app's accent again.
6. A native button, a switch and a symbol drawn in `AccentColor` inside the red node are red; the same three outside it are the app's accent.
7. An `accent-color` given as a light/dark pair follows the trait change on the node (dark mode flips it without a new batch).
8. A control with its own `color` inside the red node takes that `color` for its title (LLP 1115 D4 as edited); a control that only inherits a `color` from the page keeps the tint.

**D3, dimming** (`AppAccentIOSTests`; the default built, the row new):
9. Behind a dimmed ancestor, a native button with its own `accent-color` and one with the app's accent are both `.dimmed` (built).
10. `-exact-accent-color-adjust: normal` on a node keeps it and its subtree `.normal` behind a dimmed ancestor; a sibling without it is `.dimmed`.
11. `-exact-accent-color-adjust: dimmed` greys a subtree with no modal up.
12. Restyling `normal` back to `auto` returns the subtree to `.automatic` (it dims behind the modal again).
13. A real alert: presenting an `alertdialog` dims a button behind it, and dismissing it restores the colour (a session-level test, as `MenusIOS`'s alert tests drive one).

**Contract** (`contract/cli/tests/it`, new):
14. `-exact-accent-color-adjust` lowers `auto`, `normal` and `dimmed`, inherits, and refuses any other word naming the three.
15. `accent-color: AccentColor` stays refused (LLP 1095 D2).

**Web, both targets** (`host/web/*.test.mjs`, new):
16. `color: AccentColor` under `accent-color: red` computes red; with no authored accent it computes the browser's `AccentColor`.
17. `-exact-accent-color-adjust` writes nothing to the page.

**By hand, iOS 27 simulator, against a hand-built UIKit screen:** a root accent in an alert's buttons, a sheet's bar, a back chevron and a `UIMenu` checkmark; a red subtree beside a blue sibling; each greying behind an alert; `normal` and `dimmed` on one subtree. Screenshots in the PR.

**D4 and D5** are tested with LLP 1081 stage 2 (its §4 lists the symbol cases); D5 adds the refusal of `-exact-tint-color` on a symbol and on a raster, each naming its replacement.

## 5. Open questions for the maintainer

1. **Raster tinting (D5).** Deleting it assumes symbols and SVG cover one-colour icons. If an app needs a tinted PNG, the alternative is an explicit switch, `-exact-image-rendering-mode: original | template` (UIKit's `UIImage.RenderingMode`), drawn in `color`.
2. **Order.** D1 lands first (`feat/accent-window-tint`), then D2 and D3's row (`feat/accent-tint-cascade`). D4 and D5 land together with LLP 1081 stage 2.
